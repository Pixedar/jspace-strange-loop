# How the questions changed

The project has several experimental families. A later working version is not automatically a better demonstration of every property tested earlier. This map separates distinct results from superseded interpretations and unsuccessful mechanisms.

## What belongs where

|---|---|---|

Stable states selectively retain discourse and stance; a shared direction favors a casual voice. | Distinct empirical result, retained in [frozen_loop/](frozen_loop/). Generic inward feedback and attractors are not claimed as newly discovered phenomena. |

## The clock and the flip are different

In the chunked-reasoning system, the persistent state crosses eight segments of one thought. Asking the self-model to distinguish those segments produces a systematic rotation. Its J-space reading follows stages such as recalling, hedging and concluding at moderate pressure; strong pressure can decouple the rotation from the thought’s content.

In later attention loops, a two-state oscillation is a token-by-token flip between nearly opposite control states. It emerges under some training conditions and is often a failure of the desired richer dynamics. Its presence does not improve or replace the earlier time-code result. Conversely, the older time code does not demonstrate what the new reader-mediated dynamics do.

## What remains of persona drift

The frozen-loop role comparison and word-level transport measurements give the casual-persona observation a specific experimental basis. They are more informative than simply observing that feedback drifts or converges. However, they concern those models, prompts, lens and feedback channel. The latest attention-loop states have not been shown to encode the same persona direction.

The new short-generation probe shows that spotlight interventions change wording, including under a non-self-describing control. A few altered continuations cannot establish a persistent personality, explain the learned state in words, or transfer the old persona result to the new mechanism.

## Corrections that should travel with the work

- The old direct query-shift channel already changed predictions. The new contribution is consequences of the reader’s own shared attention, rather than the first effect of any control state on the model.
- The field statistic named `remainder_share_of_field` is a normalized ratio of squared variations, not a literal causal percentage or a bounded partition of variance.
- The picture’s reported +0.81 correlation captures a field pattern, while its raw prediction scale remains badly calibrated.
- The early comparison suggesting individual ways of moving mixed regimes. Stable rich runs grown from the same start can resemble one another.
- A negative held-out linear text-decoding R² does not prove that states are independent of text. The picture retains genre information.
- A foreign-picture transplant can persist in a best case while a same-copy earlier picture is largely restored. “Memory” without specifying the intervention would obscure that distinction.

## Relation to known mechanisms

Self-modeling can regularize a network; attention can be understood in associative-memory terms; and predictions can change what they predict. These are established research directions, with references in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md#8-relation-to-earlier-work). The project’s contribution is the particular sequence of mechanisms and controls: exposing the destination shortcut, replacing the discrimination target, and separating reader-mediated dependence, consequences for the model, and the still weak return from the model.

The [archived report](history/REPORT_20261014.md) preserves the detailed earlier account. The current report takes precedence where interpretations overlap.
