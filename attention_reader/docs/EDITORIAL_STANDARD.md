# Explanation standard used for this edition

Wiktor Tomasik · 3 November 2026

The intended reader understands scientific reasoning but has not followed this project. The failure being corrected is missing explanation, not insufficient decoration or insufficient introductory material about language models.

Each chapter has one distinct question. Each experiment explains why it discriminates between plausible mechanisms, what was held fixed and changed, what the comparison actually measures, the observed result, the inference it supports and the relevant limitation. Technical terms are defined at their first useful occurrence. A name is not an explanation: an attention forecast predicts a selected-minus-average key contrast; an interpretation model predicts a selected-minus-evenly-read picture contrast.

Every figure states what the horizontal and vertical positions mean, the unit and denominator, the reference comparison, the experimental unit and the conclusion it supports. Text supplies the reading of the plot, including a concrete example when useful. Descriptive scores, causal tests and functional claims remain distinct. Confidence changes are not rewritten as improved reasoning, and numbers from different metrics are not compared as percentages of one capacity.

The main account contains each result once. Interactive controls expose cases, variants and details where they are relevant. Recorded paths are labelled playback; geometric axes have no invented psychological meanings. Legacy results remain valid only for their tested configurations. Current results and proposed architectures are separate.

Review passes: (1) scientific provenance and scope; (2) reader perspective, asking what an unfamiliar reader can infer from each paragraph/plot; (3) repetition and terminology; (4) numerical and interface verification. This is a self-review workflow, not a claim of independent human readability testing.

The review separates sentence polishing from scientific explanation. An account can use plain words and still fail if it omits why the comparison matters or how a result supports its conclusion. The checks above therefore evaluate the logical explanation, not just wording or length.
